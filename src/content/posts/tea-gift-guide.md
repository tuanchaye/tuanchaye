In Chinese culture, tea is one of the most common and appreciated gifts. But the gift-tea market is full of overpriced tins. Here is how to give well without overspending.

## Good Gift Teas
- **Longjing or Biluochun (green):** recognizable names, safe and elegant.
- **Tie Guan Yin or Da Hong Pao (oolong):** aromatic, impressive, widely liked.
- **A small pu-erh cake:** feels special and stores well.
- **High-quality black tea (Keemun, Dian Hong):** approachable for almost anyone.

## Judge by the Leaf, Not the Box
Fancy packaging often hides mediocre tea. Look for:
- Whole, uniform leaves or buds (not dust).
- A clear harvest date and origin.
- A clean, specific aroma.

A plain bag of excellent tea beats a lacquered box of unknown blend.

## Match the Person
- New to tea? Choose mild green or black.
- Likes ritual? A gaiwan set with oolong.
- Health-conscious? White or green.
- Experienced drinker? A single-origin oolong or aged pu-erh.

## Practical Tip
Buy from a trusted specialty shop rather than a tourist counter. A 50–100 g portion of genuinely good tea is a generous, memorable gift.

The best tea gift is one the recipient will actually enjoy — so when in doubt, pick something approachable and fresh.
