Yes — tea contains caffeine. But the story is more nuanced (and more reassuring) than many people expect.

## How Much Caffeine Is in Tea?
A typical cup of tea has roughly **20–60 mg** of caffeine, compared with **80–120 mg** in a similar cup of coffee. The exact amount depends on the type and how you brew it:

- **White tea:** lowest (sometimes very low)
- **Green tea:** low to moderate
- **Oolong tea:** moderate
- **Black tea:** highest among true teas
- **Herbal "teas" (like chamomile):** naturally caffeine-free, because they are not from the tea plant

## Why Tea Feels Gentler Than Coffee
Tea contains an amino acid called **L-theanine**, which promotes calm focus and smooths out caffeine's jittery edge. Many people find tea gives steady energy without the coffee crash.

## How to Lower the Caffeine
If you are sensitive, try these tricks:

1. **Short rinse:** pour hot water over the leaves and discard it after 5–10 seconds. A significant portion of caffeine (which extracts fast) goes into that first rinse.
2. **Brew cooler and shorter:** lower temperature and time pull less caffeine.
3. **Pick white or green tea** over black.
4. **Avoid the first infusion** if you really want to minimize it.

## A Note on Health
We share general tea knowledge only and do not provide medical advice. If you have caffeine-related health concerns, please consult a qualified professional.

For most people, moderate tea drinking is a pleasant, low-caffeine way to stay alert through the day.
