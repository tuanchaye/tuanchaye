Strictly speaking, "tea" comes only from the *Camellia sinensis* plant. But across China, people drink many **tisanes** — infusions of flowers, fruits, and herbs — that are part of daily life. Here are three classics.

## Chrysanthemum (菊花, Ju Hua)
Dried chrysanthemum flowers steep into a pale, floral, slightly sweet cup. Often paired with goji berries or a touch of rock sugar. Served cool in summer or warm year-round. It is the most common "flower tea" in China.

## Goji Berry (枸杞, Gou Qi)
Tiny red berries, mildly sweet and chewy, rich in color. Usually added to green tea or chrysanthemum, or steeped alone. A spoonful per cup is enough.

## Jujube / Red Date (红枣, Hong Zao)
Dried red dates, sliced and steeped, give a warm, sweet, almost caramel-like infusion. Popular in winter and in nourishing blends.

## How to Brew Them
These are forgiving: use **90–100°C** water, steep 5–10 minutes, and re-infuse. They are naturally caffeine-free, so they suit evenings.

## A Note
We share general knowledge about traditional drinks only and do not provide medical or health advice. If you have health conditions or take medication, consult a qualified professional before using any herbal infusion regularly.

Think of these as the caffeine-free cousins of true tea — pleasant, flexible, and a gentle way to explore Chinese drinking culture beyond the six tea types.
