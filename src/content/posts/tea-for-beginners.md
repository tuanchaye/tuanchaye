Chinese tea looks intimidating, but you can enjoy it with almost no equipment. Here is a clear roadmap.

## Step 1: Pick One Easy Tea
Start with **green tea (Longjing)** or a **gentle oolong (Tie Guan Yin)**. Avoid chasing rare or expensive teas early.

## Step 2: Get a Basic Vessel
A **porcelain gaiwan** (100–150 ml) or even a glass mug is enough. Skip elaborate sets.

## Step 3: Control Two Variables
Just two things decide most of your results:
- **Water temperature** (cooler for green/white, hot for oolong/black/pu-erh)
- **Steep time** (shorter = less bitter)

## Step 4: Taste Deliberately
Brew the same tea at different times. Notice how 30 seconds vs 3 minutes changes everything. Build your aroma memory.

## Step 5: Expand Slowly
Once you like one tea, try its neighbors: from green, explore white and yellow; from oolong, explore black and pu-erh.

## What You Do NOT Need
- An expensive teapot
- A scale (eyeball 1 teaspoon per cup to start)
- Dozens of teas at once

## The Mindset
Chinese tea is less about rules and more about *attention*. Brew, taste, adjust, repeat. Within a month you will know more than most casual drinkers.

Start today with one good tea and one cup. That is the whole roadmap.
