Ask any tea drinker what ruins a good leaf and you will hear: *wrong water temperature.* Too hot scalds delicate teas bitter; too cool leaves bold teas flat. Here is the practical chart.

## Temperature by Type
| Tea type | Temperature | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| White tea | 80–90°C | Tender buds; cooler is safer |
| Green tea | 75–85°C | Above 85°C turns bitter |
| Yellow tea | 80–85°C | Like a gentle green |
| Oolong (light) | 90–95°C | |
| Oolong (dark/roasted) | 95–100°C | Loves heat |
| Black tea (hong cha) | 90–100°C | Robust; boiling fine |
| Pu-erh (raw & ripe) | 95–100°C | Needs heat to open |
| Matcha | 75–80°C | Never boiling |

## No Thermometer?
- **Off the boil 30–60 seconds** ≈ 90–95°C (good for oolong/black).
- **Off the boil 2–3 minutes** ≈ 80°C (good for green/white).
- Let it cool longer for the most delicate buds.

## Why It Matters
Heat extracts bitterness and astringency faster than sweetness. Delicate teas need a gentle touch; bold, rolled, or aged teas need real heat to unfold. Once you match temperature to leaf, weak or bitter cups largely disappear.

Keep this chart near your kettle — it is the single biggest upgrade to your brewing after leaf quality.
