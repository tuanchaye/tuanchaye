Pu-erh (普洱茶) from Yunnan is the most unusual member of the six major tea types. Unlike green or black tea, pu-erh is **post-fermented** — after basic processing, living microbes continue to transform the leaves over years or even decades.

## Two Kinds of Pu-erh
- **Sheng (生茶, "raw"):** aged naturally. When young it is bright and astringent; with age it mellows into deep, complex, honeyed flavors. Collectors prize old sheng.
- **Shou (熟茶, "ripe"):** sped up with a controlled fermentation ("wet piling") so it tastes aged right away — smooth, earthy, and comforting.

## Why People Love It
- **Depth:** a well-aged pu-erh has notes of wood, dried fruit, mushroom, and sweet earth that no other tea offers.
- **Digestibility:** many drink it after heavy meals; it is famously gentle on the stomach.
- **Longevity:** properly stored cakes can improve for 20+ years.

## How to Brew Pu-erh
Use a **gaiwan or small clay pot**, ~5–7 g per 100 ml, water at 95–100°C. Always do a quick **rinse** (5–10 seconds, discard) to "wake" the leaves, then steep just a few seconds and re-infuse many times.

## How to Start
Buy a small **cake or tuo cha** (compressed disc) of shou pu-erh from a reputable source — it is the easiest entry. Explore sheng later once your palate is ready.
