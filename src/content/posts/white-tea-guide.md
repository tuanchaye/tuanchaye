White tea (白茶) is the closest thing to tea leaves in their natural state. It skips rolling and pan-firing almost entirely — the leaves are simply **withered** in fresh air and then dried. That minimal handling is exactly why white tea tastes so subtle.

## What Does It Taste Like?
Expect light, sweet, and floral notes with a soft, silky mouthfeel. There is no bitterness when brewed correctly. Young white tea is fresh and melon-like; aged white tea (stored for years) develops deeper, honeyed, almost medicinal warmth prized by collectors.

## The Main Varieties
- **Silver Needle (白毫银针):** only buds, covered in fine white down. The most premium and delicate.
- **White Peony (白牡丹):** buds plus young leaves. More body, still gentle.
- **Shou Mei (寿眉):** mature leaves, bolder and more affordable — great for everyday drinking and aging.

## How to Brew White Tea
Because the leaves are tender, avoid boiling water:

- **Temperature:** 80–90°C
- **Amount:** 3–4 g per 250 ml
- **Steeping:** 3–5 minutes; it can be re-steeped several times

You can also cold-brew white tea overnight in the fridge for a naturally sweet, low-caffeine cup.

## Why Try It?
White tea is the perfect entry point for people who find green tea too "grassy" or who want a calming, low-caffeine drink. It also showcases how much flavor comes purely from the leaf itself, with almost no intervention.
