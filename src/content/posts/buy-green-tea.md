Green tea is the most popular tea in China, but quality varies enormously. The good news: you do not need to spend a fortune to drink well — you need to know what to look for.

## Freshness First
Green tea is best within the **same year it is picked**, especially spring tea (明前/雨前). Buy from sellers who state the harvest date. Old or poorly stored green tea loses its aroma and turns dull.

## Look at the Leaf
Good green tea has:
- **Even, intact leaves or buds** — not broken dust
- **A lively green color** — yellowing means age or poor storage
- **A clean, grassy or nutty aroma** — no mustiness

## Classic Beginner-Friendly Varieties
- **Longjing (Dragon Well, 龙井):** flat, pan-fired, sweet and chestnut-like. The most famous Chinese green.
- **Biluochun (碧螺春):** curly, tender, floral and fruity.
- **Gunpowder (珠茶):** rolled pellets, robust, great with food and very affordable.

## Buy Loose, Not Bags
Whole-leaf loose tea almost always tastes better than dust-filled tea bags. A 50–100 g bag is plenty to start.

## Storage
Keep green tea **sealed, away from light, heat, and odors** — the fridge (in an airtight container) is ideal for long-term freshness.

Start with one well-known variety from a trusted shop, learn its taste, then branch out. Taste memory is your best buying guide.
