In much of the world, tea is the default table drink — and for good reason. It cleanses the palate, cuts richness, and adds its own aroma to a meal. Here is a simple way to pair it.

## With Savory and Fried Foods
**Green or oolong tea** cuts grease and refreshes. A smoky Lapsang or roasted oolong stands up to grilled and fried dishes.

## With Sweet Desserts
**Black tea (hong cha)** matches sweetness; a malty Keemun or Dian Hong pairs beautifully with milk-based or chocolate desserts. White tea is lovely with fruit.

## With Spicy Food
Cool, light **white or green tea** soothes heat better than strong tea. Avoid very astringent teas, which can amplify spice.

## With Dim Sum and Light Meals
**Oolong** is the classic Cantonese choice — fragrant enough to complement, gentle enough not to overwhelm.

## The Principle: Contrast and Cleanse
Where wine often mirrors food (rich with rich), tea usually *contrasts* — a bright, clean cup resets the palate between bites. That is why it works across so many cuisines.

## A Practical Start
Keep a versatile oolong and a fresh green tea on hand; between them you can pair almost any everyday meal. Notice how the same food tastes different with each — that discovery is most of the fun.
