Matcha (抹茶) is green tea in its most concentrated form: whole leaves, shade-grown, de-stemmed, and stone-ground into a fine powder that you whisk and drink entirely.

## Why Shade-Grown Matters
Weeks before harvest, the plants are shaded. This boosts chlorophyll and amino acids (especially L-theanine), giving matcha its vivid color and sweet, umami-rich taste rather than bitterness.

## Ceremonial vs Culinary
- **Ceremonial grade:** made from the youngest leaves, smooth, sweet, no bitterness. Drink it plain with water.
- **Culinary grade:** stronger and more astringent, intended for lattes, baking, and cooking.

For drinking, buy ceremonial grade from a source that states the harvest and origin (Uji or Nishio in Japan are classic regions).

## How to Prepare
1. Sift 1–2 g (about 1 teaspoon) of matcha into a bowl.
2. Add ~70 ml of water at **75–80°C** (not boiling — it scorches).
3. Whisk in a quick "W" or "M" motion with a bamboo whisk until a fine foam forms.

## A Note on Caffeine
Because you consume the whole leaf, matcha contains more caffeine and more antioxidants than a cup of steeped green tea — a little goes a long way.

Matcha is less about casual sipping and more about a focused, energizing ritual. Start with a small tin of good ceremonial grade and a bamboo whisk.
