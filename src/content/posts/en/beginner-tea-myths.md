Starting tea can be confusing because so much advice is contradictory. Here are five common myths — and the practical truth.

## Myth 1: "You must use boiling water for all tea."
False. Delicate green and white teas scorch above ~80–85°C and turn bitter. Use cooler water for light teas, near-boiling for oolong, black, and pu-erh.

## Myth 2: "The first infusion should always be thrown away."
Partly true for **pu-erh and some oolong** (a quick rinse wakes the leaves). For green or white tea, the first steep is often the best — drink it.

## Myth 3: "Darker tea has more caffeine."
Not reliably. Caffeine depends on the type and how you brew, not just color. White tea buds can be low; black tea is usually highest among true teas.

## Myth 4: "Expensive = better."
Not always. Freshness, origin, and your own taste matter more than price. A humble but fresh green tea can outshine an old premium one.

## Myth 5: "Tea detoxes your body."
There is no scientific basis for "detox" claims. Tea is a pleasant, mildly stimulating drink — not a cure. We share general knowledge only, not medical advice.

## The Takeaway
Trust your own palate over rules. Brew gently, taste often, and ignore anyone who says there is only one right way.
