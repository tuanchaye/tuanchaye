The vessel you brew in is not just a container — its material subtly shapes flavor, heat, and ritual. Here is how the main options compare.

## Porcelain (瓷)
- **Neutral:** does not absorb flavor, so one pot suits all teas.
- **Great heat retention** and easy to clean.
- The classic choice for gaiwans and most everyday pots.
- *Best for:* everything, especially delicate greens and whites.

## Yixing Clay / Zisha (紫砂)
- **Porous:** gradually absorbs oils and aroma, "seasoning" over time.
- Traditionally **dedicated to one tea type**.
- Beautiful and collectible, but easy to fake and pricier.
- *Best for:* oolong, pu-erh, aged teas.

## Glass (玻璃)
- Fully neutral and **transparent** — perfect for watching buds unfurl.
- Poor heat retention, so pre-warm it.
- *Best for:* green tea, flower teas, cold brew.

## Cast Iron (铁壶/铁壶急须)
- Retains heat exceptionally well; often used to *boil* water (tetsubin) rather than brew.
- Enamel-lined interiors avoid rust.
- *Best for:* keeping black/pu-erh hot during long sessions.

## How to Choose
Start with **porcelain** for versatility. Add glass for visual teas, clay for a favorite type, and cast iron only if you love long, hot sessions. Material is the quiet partner to leaf quality — get it roughly right and your tea sings.
