All true tea comes from a single species: *Camellia sinensis*. What separates white, green, yellow, oolong, black, and dark tea is not the plant, but **how the leaves are processed** after picking. The level of oxidation — and a few special steps — is what creates six dramatically different cups.

## 1. White tea — the least processed

White tea is made from young buds and tender leaves that are simply withered and dried. Almost no processing means the most delicate flavor: light, sweet, and floral. It is best brewed with cooler water (around 80°C / 175°F) for a few minutes.

## 2. Green tea — fixed before oxidation

Right after picking, green tea leaves are heated (pan-fired or steamed) to stop oxidation. That keeps the leaves green and the flavor fresh, grassy, and sometimes vegetal or umami. Brew it at 70–80°C (160–175°F) to avoid bitterness.

## 3. Yellow tea — a gentle "sealing" step

Yellow tea is a rare cousin of green tea. After fixation it goes through a short, humid "men huang" (sealing yellow) step that mellows the grassy notes into a smoother, rounder cup. Treat it like green tea when brewing.

## 4. Oolong — partially oxidized

Oolong sits between green and black: leaves are bruised and allowed to oxidize anywhere from 10% to 80%. The result ranges from floral and light to deep and roasted. Higher-oxidation oolongs like hotter water (90–100°C / 195–212°F) and can be re-steeped many times.

## 5. Black tea — fully oxidized

Black tea is fully oxidized, which turns the leaves dark and develops bold, malty, fruity, or brisk flavors. It takes the hottest water (90–100°C / 195–212°F) and forms the base of English breakfast and milk teas.

## 6. Dark tea (Pu-erh) — post-fermented

Dark tea is unique: after basic processing it is fermented by microbes and often aged for years. Pu-erh is the best-known example. The flavor is earthy, smooth, and mellow, and it is traditionally brewed with near-boiling water, sometimes after a quick rinse of the leaves.

## How to choose as a beginner

- Prefer delicate, light flavors? Start with **white** or **green**.
- Like something aromatic and complex? Try a light **oolong**.
- Enjoy bold, robust cups (with or without milk)? Go for **black**.
- Curious about aged, earthy teas? Explore **dark (Pu-erh)**.

No matter the type, fresh leaves, good water, and the right temperature will take you far. Pick one, brew a cup, and let your taste guide the next.
