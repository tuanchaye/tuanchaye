Oolong is one of the most rewarding teas to brew properly — but the method is different from a Western mug. The secret is **small amounts of leaf, short steeps, many infusions**.

## What You Need
- A **gaiwan** or small clay pot (100–150 ml)
- Oolong leaf: **5–7 g** (a generous heap — oolong expands)
- Water at **90–100°C**

## The Steps
1. **Warm** the gaiwan and cups with hot water, then discard.
2. **Add leaf** and give it a quick rinse (2–3 seconds, discard) to open the leaves.
3. **Steep** the first infusion just **10–20 seconds**.
4. **Pour completely** into a sharing pitcher or cups — don't let it sit.
5. **Repeat**, adding a few seconds each time. Good oolong gives 5–8 infusions.

## Why So Short?
Oolong is rolled tight and flavor-dense. A long steep turns it bitter and astringent. Short steeps let each round reveal a different note — floral, then creamy, then roasted.

## For a Western-Style Cup
No gaiwan? Use ~3 g per 250 ml at 95°C, steep 3 minutes. It will not be as layered, but still delicious.

## Tip
Light oolongs (Tie Guan Yin) shine at the cooler end; dark oolongs (Da Hong Pao) love full heat. Once you taste the difference short steeps make, you will rarely go back to the mug.
