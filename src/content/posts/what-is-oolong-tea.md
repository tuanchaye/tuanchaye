Oolong (乌龙茶) is a traditional Chinese tea that is partially oxidized —
somewhere between unoxidized green tea and fully oxidized black tea. This
in-between character gives oolong an extraordinary range of aromas, from
floral and creamy to roasted and fruity.

## How Oolong Is Made

The defining step is **bruising the leaves** to start oxidation, then halting
it at just the right moment by pan-firing or roasting. Skilled tea makers
repeatedly roll and oxidize the leaves to shape them into tight balls or
twisted strips.

## Flavor Profiles

- **Light oolong** (e.g. Tie Guan Yin): floral, fresh, buttery
- **Medium oolong** (e.g. Dan Cong): honeyed, orchid-like
- **Dark oolong** (e.g. Da Hong Pao): roasted, mineral, deep

## Why It Matters

Oolong is central to Chinese tea ceremony culture, especially in Fujian and
Guangdong. For beginners, it is a wonderful gateway into the complexity of
Chinese tea.
