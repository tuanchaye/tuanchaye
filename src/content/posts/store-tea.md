Tea is a natural agricultural product, and it slowly loses aroma after harvest. The difference between "fine" and "flat" tea is often just storage. The rule is simple: protect it from **light, air, heat, and moisture**.

## The Four Enemies
1. **Light** — breaks down aroma compounds. Keep tea in opaque containers.
2. **Air (oxygen)** — causes staleness. Use airtight seals.
3. **Heat** — speeds aging. Cool storage is better.
4. **Moisture** — the worst: it causes mold and off-flavors. Always keep dry.

## A Simple Method That Works
- Put loose tea in an **opaque, airtight tin or resealable pouch**.
- Squeeze out excess air before sealing.
- Store in a **cool, dark cupboard**.

## When to Use the Fridge
For **green and white tea** (delicate, unoxidized), the refrigerator is ideal:
- Use a **truly airtight container** — moisture from the fridge will ruin tea otherwise.
- Let it **return to room temperature** before opening, so condensation does not form on the leaves.
- Keep it away from strong-smelling foods.

**Black, oolong, and pu-erh** do not need the fridge; a cool cupboard is fine. Pu-erh and dark teas actually *benefit* from slow aging in stable conditions — but still keep them dry and away from odors.

## How Long Does Tea Last?
- Green/white: best within a year, especially the same harvest year.
- Black/oolong: 1–2 years if sealed.
- Pu-erh: decades, by design.

Buy in amounts you will finish within a season, and your tea will always taste its best.
