One of the first questions new tea drinkers ask is: *what should I brew with?* The two most common Chinese vessels are the **gaiwan** and the **clay teapot (紫砂壶)**. Here is how to choose.

## The Gaiwan (盖碗)
A gaiwan is a lidded bowl, usually porcelain, with a saucer. It looks simple but is the most versatile tool in Chinese tea:

- **Neutral:** porcelain does not absorb flavor, so one gaiwan suits every tea.
- **Affordable:** a decent gaiwan costs far less than a good clay pot.
- **Educational:** you see the leaves, control the steep time, and learn how tea behaves.

For most beginners, **start with a gaiwan.** A 100–150 ml porcelain gaiwan is ideal.

## The Clay Teapot (Zisha)
Yixing (宜兴) purple-clay teapots are beloved for their beauty and the way they "season" over time, absorbing a little flavor from each brew. But they have rules:

- Traditionally dedicated to **one type of tea** (e.g. only oolong, only pu-erh).
- More expensive and easier to fake.
- Best appreciated after you already know what you like.

## Practical Tips
- Look for a gaiwan with a comfortable rim that does not trap heat against your fingers.
- Avoid "kung fu" sets with tiny cups until you know you enjoy the ritual.
- Glass and ceramic mugs work fine too — equipment is far less important than leaf quality and water temperature.

**Bottom line:** buy a porcelain gaiwan first. Add a clay teapot later, once a specific tea has won your heart.
