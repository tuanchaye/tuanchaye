Yellow tea (黄茶) is the rarest and least known of China's six major tea types. If you have never tasted it, you are not alone — even in China it is hard to find.

## What Makes It Yellow?
Like green tea, the leaves are not oxidized. The difference is a special step called **"men huang" (闷黄, sealing yellow)**: after initial heating, the leaves are piled and gently wrapped while still warm and moist. This slow, humid resting softens the grassy edge of green tea, producing a rounder, sweeter, corn-like flavor and a pale yellow liquor.

## Famous Varieties
- **Junshan Yinzhen (君山银针):** from Hunan, made of single buds that stand upright when brewed — a famous, delicate yellow tea.
- **Mengding Huangya (蒙顶黄芽):** from Sichuan, tender buds, sweet and smooth.
- **Huoshan Huangya (霍山黄芽):** from Anhui, affordable and approachable.

## How to Brew
Treat it like a gentle green tea: water at **80–85°C**, ~3 g per 150 ml, steep 2–3 minutes. A glass cup lets you watch the buds dance.

## Why Try It?
Yellow tea is the bridge between green and white — fresher than oolong, smoother than green. Because it is rare and often mislabeled (many "yellow" teas are just green teas with a longer fixation), finding a genuine one is part of the fun.

For collectors and curious drinkers, yellow tea is the missing piece that completes the map of China's six tea types.
