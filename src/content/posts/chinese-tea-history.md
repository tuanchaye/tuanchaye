Tea is inseparable from Chinese history. You do not need the details to enjoy a cup, but a quick timeline adds depth to every sip.

## Legendary Beginnings
The most famous story credits Emperor Shennong (神农), thousands of years ago, with discovering tea when leaves blew into his boiling water. Whether true or not, it captures how ancient the habit is.

## The Tang Dynasty (618–907)
Tea became a refined drink. **Lu Yu's "Classic of Tea" (茶经)** — the world's first tea treatise — set standards for growing, making, and drinking. Tea was pressed into cakes.

## The Song Dynasty (960–1279)
Powdered tea whisked in bowls became an art form (the ancestor of matcha). Elegant tea competitions flourished among the literati.

## The Ming Dynasty (1368–1644)
A turning point: the emperor banned compressed cakes in favor of **loose leaf**, brewed by steeping — the method we use today. This gave rise to the great loose-leaf traditions of green, oolong, and black tea.

## Going Global
From the 16th century, tea traveled to Europe and beyond, eventually becoming one of the world's most traded commodities and reshaping economies.

## Why It Matters
Chinese tea is not just a beverage — it is a continuous 1,000-year conversation about craft, ritual, and calm. When you brew a gaiwan of oolong, you join that line.
