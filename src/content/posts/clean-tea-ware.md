Good tea ware lasts for years, but only if you care for it simply and consistently. The good news: less is more.

## After Every Session
- **Rinse with plain hot water** immediately after use — no soap needed.
- **Wipe and air-dry** thoroughly. Trapped moisture causes odors and stains.
- Avoid dishwashers and detergents; they leave residues that ruin tea flavor.

## Porcelain and Glass (Gaiwan, Cups)
These are easy. Hot water rinse, occasional gentle scrub with a soft brush if stained. They stay neutral, so they suit every tea.

## Clay Teapots (Zisha)
Clay is porous and "seasoned" by the teas you brew:
- **Dedicate one pot to one type of tea** (e.g. only oolong).
- **Never use soap.** A rinse and a soft brush are enough.
- Over time a patina builds that many enthusiasts value.
- If a pot smells off, fill with boiling water, let cool, rinse, repeat.

## Dealing with Stains and Odors
- Baking soda paste works for stubborn porcelain stains.
- For smell, a few rounds of boiling-water rinses usually solve it.
- Store ware **dry and uncovered** so it can breathe.

## The One Rule
**Rinse hot, dry completely, skip the soap.** Follow that and your tea ware will taste clean and look beautiful for a long time.
