Longjing (龙井, "Dragon Well") from Hangzhou is the green tea by which others are judged in China — smooth, flat, and unmistakably elegant.

## What It Looks Like
The leaves are **flat and smooth**, pressed straight during pan-firing (a defining step that gives Longjing its toasty, chestnut sweetness and stops oxidation). The brewed cup is a clear, light green-yellow.

## Flavor
- **Sweet and nutty** (think toasted chestnut)
- **Smooth, almost no bitterness** when brewed correctly
- A clean, lingering finish

## How to Brew
Water at **75–80°C** (never boiling), ~3 g per 150 ml, steep 1–2 minutes. A glass cup shows the leaves standing upright — part of the charm.

## Buying Tips
- Look for **Xi Hu** (West Lake) region for the premium version, though neighboring areas make good everyday Longjing too.
- Freshness is everything: buy the current spring harvest.
- Beware very cheap "Longjing" — much is imitation from other provinces.

## Why It Matters
Longjing is the gateway green tea: forgiving, fragrant, and quintessentially Chinese. Mastering its gentle brewing is a small but satisfying step into the world of fine tea.
