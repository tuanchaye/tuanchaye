You do not need a trained palate to taste tea well — you need *attention*. Here is a simple framework anyone can use.

## 1. Look (汤色)
Pour the tea and observe the liquor:
- **Clarity:** good tea is bright and clear, never cloudy (unless intentionally cloudy like some matcha).
- **Color:** pale green, golden, amber, or deep red — each type has its range.

## 2. Smell (香气)
Inhale before and after sipping:
- **Dry leaf aroma** tells you about processing.
- **Wet leaf and cup aroma** reveals the true character — floral, roasted, fruity, marine.
- Slurp gently to aerosolize the aroma into your nose.

## 3. Taste (滋味)
Notice on the tongue:
- **Sweetness** vs **bitterness/astringency**
- **Body** — thin, medium, or full
- **Aftertaste (回甘)** — a pleasant sweetness that returns after swallowing; a hallmark of quality

## 4. Feel (体感)
Good tea feels smooth, not rough. A clean, lingering finish matters more than a strong first hit.

## Practice Tip
Taste the same tea side by side at different steep times, or compare two origins. Contrast teaches faster than solitary sipping.

Over weeks, your memory for aromas grows. The goal is not to sound expert — it is to know what *you* like and why. That is real tea literacy.
